## Methodology and its limits

- **Tool used**: a generic web-search tool only (no Google Ads/Keyword Planner login, no SEMrush/Ahrefs/Moz, no Search Console or Analytics access to MeltGrid's own historical data).
- **What was actually measured**: for each keyword cluster, I ran live searches and read the resulting search-result-page content — what kind of pages rank (vendor/manufacturer sites like WarmlyYours and Warmup, aggregator/price-guide sites like HomeStars, Angi, HomeAdvisor, LawnStarter, or named local GTA competitors like Action Home Services, GTA Landscaping, OAKS Home Services, MVR Landscaping, Artech Landscaping, A Plus Quality, HAMCO), how many dedicated competitor pages target a phrase, how specific and commercially-worded the surrounding content was, and what related/adjacent questions and terminology repeatedly surfaced (e.g., "interlock" vs. "pavers," "glycol" vs. "hydronic," tire-track vs. full-coverage heating, panel-amperage sizing). That observed pattern is what the `seo_opportunity_qualitative` column describes, and it is explicitly labeled throughout as a qualitative SEO-opportunity signal, not a search-volume metric.
- **What was NOT measured, anywhere in this deliverable**: actual monthly search counts, a numeric competition index, or real CPC/bid ranges. No number of that kind appears in the CSV. Where cost figures appear in this notes file or were referenced during research (e.g., "$10–$25/sqft," "$6,000–$20,000 installed"), those are **third-party installation/operating cost estimates pulled from contractor and vendor guides** — they describe what a heated driveway costs to build or run, not how often people search for it or what it costs to advertise against it in Google Ads. The two should not be confused.
- **Site review**: I read the titles, meta descriptions, H1s, and body text of MeltGrid's own extracted site (`/heated-driveways/`, `/residential/`, `/commercial/`, `/cost/`, `/technology/`, `/how-it-works/`, the `/resources/` sub-pages, `/snow-melting/asphalt/`, `/snow-melting/concrete/`, `/snow-melting/pavers/`, `/trade-partners/`, `/about/`) to map every keyword to an existing page where one genuinely fits, rather than guessing from the URL list alone. One notable finding from that review: **the current MeltGrid site contains zero mentions of "hydronic," "boiler," or "glycol"** — it is built entirely around electric mat/cable technology (WarmlyYours). Category B (hydronic) keywords are included per the brief's seed list and because real searcher and competitor demand for hydronic terminology clearly exists in the GTA market, but they currently map to "none" or to a *new* section on `/technology/` rather than an existing MeltGrid page — this is a real content gap to flag to the business owner, not an oversight in the mapping.
- **Electric vs. hydronic terminology observed**: searchers and press use a mix of technical terms ("hydronic," "boiler-fed") and plainer language ("hot water heated driveway," "glycol system," "water-and-antifreeze mix"). Competitor marketing in the GTA uses "glycol" specifically. This is noted directly in the relevant rows' `seo_opportunity_qualitative` column rather than asserted as a volume fact.

## Clustering logic — which keywords share a target page vs. need their own

The `recommended_target_url` column reflects this logic directly; the summary is:

1. **Pillar/core pages absorb broad synonyms.** `/heated-driveways/` is the target for the broadest, most generic terms ("heated driveway," "radiant heated driveway," "heated driveway GTA") rather than creating thin new pages for each synonym. Likewise `/technology/` absorbs every mats-vs-cable, sensor, and controls variant; `/how-it-works/` absorbs every "installation process" / "how does it work" phrasing.
2. **Existing dedicated pages get the exact-match keyword plus its close variants**, not a new page. Example: `heated driveway electrical requirements`, `electric heated driveway panel amps`, and `heated driveway panel upgrade` all map to the single existing `/resources/electrical-requirements/` page rather than three separate pages, because the search results for all three returned the same underlying content cluster (watts-per-sqft sizing, amperage, panel-upgrade cost). The same logic applies to the retrofit resource page (`heated driveway retrofit`, `heated driveway replacement`, `snow melting cable in existing asphalt`) and the running-cost resource page (`heated driveway running cost`, `electricity consumption`, `hydronic snow melting operating cost`).
3. **Surface pages (asphalt/concrete/pavers) absorb their surface-specific long tail**, including the Ontario-specific "interlock" terminology for pavers, which is a genuinely distinct regional phrasing worth targeting explicitly on `/snow-melting/pavers/` rather than relying on "pavers" alone.
4. **A single comparison asset serves multiple near-duplicate "vs." queries.** "Hydronic vs. electric heated driveway" and "electric vs. hydronic snow melting" are different phrasings of the same decision-stage question and should be answered by one well-built comparison page/section, not two.
5. **New content is recommended only where no existing page fits and the gap is real**, not for every keyword. The clearest cases: a hydronic/glycol explainer (none of the current site covers it at all), a heated-wheelchair-ramp/accessibility section (genuinely underserved in the wider market, not just on MeltGrid's site), and a commercial-cost explainer (every source researched explicitly said reliable commercial pricing isn't available online — a real, fillable gap).
6. **Near-duplicate phrasings are flagged as low incremental value rather than given separate rows of effort.** Several rows (e.g., "heated front steps" vs. "heated outdoor stairs," "heated garage ramp" vs. "heated parking garage ramp") are kept as separate CSV rows per the brief's request for keyword *candidates*, but their `suggested_content_format` and `business_value` explicitly say to fold them into one shared content block rather than building duplicate pages.

## City-page recommendation

**Recommendation: build one strong GTA/Ontario regional page (reuse the existing `/heated-driveways/` pillar page, with a clear service-area section), not a separate landing page per suburb.** Within that regional page, give **Toronto, Vaughan, Richmond Hill, and Mississauga** slightly more specific treatment (a short paragraph and/or real local proof point each) than the remaining cities, which should simply be listed in a general service-area list.

Rationale, based only on what actually surfaced in live searches (not assumption):

- **"GTA" is the term the market actually uses**, not individual city names. Every named competitor (Action Home Services, GTA Landscaping, OAKS Home Services, MVR Landscaping, Artech Landscaping, A Plus Quality) brands itself around "Toronto and the GTA" or a short cluster of cities, not single-city pages. This is the single strongest and most consistent signal across every location search run.
- **Toronto** is the clear anchor: it has its own dedicated local press coverage (BlogTO, Toronto Life, Toronto Mike, a Globe and Mail article), a WarmlyYours Toronto-specific case study, and the deepest competitor presence of any city searched. It should be MeltGrid's primary location-branded page/section.
- **Vaughan** produced a genuine, specific project proof point (a WarmlyYours case study of an asphalt driveway + stairs, plus an independent HomeStars review citing a roughly $12,000 interlocking-driveway project) — real, citable local evidence, even if thinner than Toronto's.
- **Richmond Hill** is notable mainly because the most visible single competitor (Action Home Services) is headquartered there — a competitive-presence signal worth acknowledging, more than an independent demand signal.
- **Mississauga** was the only other city with a distinct, city-specific detail (a municipal requirement that paving contractors hold an annually-renewed city trade licence) plus multiple named competitors explicitly listing it in their service area.
- **Markham, Brampton, Oakville, Burlington, Aurora, Newmarket, King City, and Caledon** returned no dedicated competitor pages, no local press, and no independent case studies or reviews distinct from the general GTA cluster in the searches run. Oakville and Burlington appeared only as entries in other companies' multi-city service lists (grouped with Mississauga/Hamilton), not as their own cluster. Building a dedicated landing page for each of these, with no supporting evidence of distinct search behavior or local competitor activity, would likely produce a set of thin, hard-to-differentiate pages — exactly the near-duplicate city-page pattern the research brief asked to avoid. A single bullet or line in a general "we also serve..." service-area list is the appropriate treatment for these eight.

This recommendation should be revisited once real Keyword Planner data (by city, if that granularity is available) comes back — it is possible one or two of the "generic list" cities show meaningfully higher real volume than the others once actual numbers exist.
