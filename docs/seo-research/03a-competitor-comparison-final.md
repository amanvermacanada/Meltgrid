## Specific weaknesses MeltGrid can realistically outperform

These are the gaps where the fix is content/positioning MeltGrid controls directly — not something that requires years of accumulated reviews to close:

1. **MSD Contracting's apparent placeholder phone number** on its own service page — a live, fixable defect any competitor audit of MeltGrid's own (carefully QA'd) CTAs already compares favourably against.
2. **MKC Group and Northpoint Plumbing's thin, FAQ-less service-menu pages** — MeltGrid's existing page depth and FAQ/schema coverage already out-executes these on content alone; the only reason they could still outrank MeltGrid today is MeltGrid's lack of index age, not page quality.
3. **EC Mechanical's self-contradicting experience claims** (10 years on one page, 15 on another) — a specific, named inconsistency; MeltGrid's own claims should stay internally consistent as hydronic content is built out.
4. **No competitor combines genuine depth on both electric AND hydronic with an explicit multi-trade coordination story.** EC Mechanical, Toronto Air Systems, The Boiler Guys and InFloor Heat are hydronic-only; Country Renovations is electrician-led. This is the one positioning gap every other competitor leaves open, and it's the one MeltGrid's actual business model (not just its marketing) can legitimately fill once hydronic content exists.
5. **No competitor publishes commercial pricing or dedicated commercial property-type content** (parking ramps, loading docks, hospital entrances) — a low-volume but genuinely open informational gap across the entire market, not just against MeltGrid.

## Where MeltGrid still has real, not-easily-closed gaps

Unchanged from the original audit: review/trust-signal volume (AHS), city-level page footprint (Precision Landscaping), published pricing specificity (Precision Landscaping), and direct-employment licensing claims (Country Renovations). None of these are solved by better copywriting — they require either an operational change (collecting reviews, publishing real ranges) or accepting the coordination-model trade-off (MeltGrid cannot claim to employ licensed trades the way Country Renovations does, and shouldn't).
